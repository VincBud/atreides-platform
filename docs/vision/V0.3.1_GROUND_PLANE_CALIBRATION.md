# V0.3.1 — Ground-Plane / Spatial Calibration

**Status:** implemented · **Substep:** V0.3.1 of V0.3 · **Not production ready**

## Purpose

V0.3.0 measured behaviour in normalized image coordinates — resolution
independent, but **camera-local**. A frame fraction is not a distance. V0.3.1
converts camera-local image positions into a **venue-local 2D coordinate system
measured in metres**, shared by every camera in a venue.

The milestone is geometry only. It transforms **positions, not identities**.

## Why normalized image coordinates are insufficient

A pixel means something different in every shot, and in every part of every
shot. The same person walking the same speed produces:

- **5× more pixels per second** at 1920 wide than at 384 wide — V0.3.0's
  normalization fixed this one;
- **far more image movement near the camera than far from it** — normalization
  does *not* fix this, and cannot;
- different values again on a camera mounted higher, angled differently, or
  fitted with a wider lens.

The second point is the one that forced this milestone. It is measured directly
in `TestPerspective`: a person walking **exactly one metre in one second** on a
synthetic obliquely-mounted camera registers

| Depth in scene | Normalized image speed | Calibrated ground-plane speed |
|---|---|---|
| Near the camera (`y = 0.5 m`) | `0.043605` frame-widths/s | **1.000000 m/s** |
| Far from the camera (`y = 8.5 m`) | `0.020604` frame-widths/s | **1.000000 m/s** |
| **Ratio** | **2.116×** | **1.000000×** |

Same person, same metre, same second — and image-space measurement disagrees
with itself by a factor of two, purely because of where they were standing. Any
threshold built on that value silently encodes one camera and one spot on its
floor.

After calibration both recover 1.000 m/s.

## Architecture

```text
image anchor → GroundPlaneProjector → MotionPoint(GROUND_PLANE_METRES) → history → kinematics → signals in m/s
```

```text
services/vision/src/atreides_vision/calibration/
├── __init__.py       # package boundary, scope and identity statement
├── homography.py     # pure array maths: normalized DLT, apply, invert
├── models.py         # VenuePoint, ReferenceFrame, PointCorrespondence,
│                     # GroundPlaneCalibration, CalibrationQuality
├── fitting.py        # CalibrationFit — solve + measure + safeguard
├── projector.py      # GroundPlaneProjector (implements MotionProjector)
└── loader.py         # JSON loading, separate from the maths
```

Layering is strict and one-directional: `homography` knows only arrays,
`models` knows only points, `fitting` joins them, `projector` adapts the result
to the behaviour protocol, `loader` handles files.

**`calibration` depends on `behavior.coordinates`; `behavior` never imports
`calibration`.** That is what keeps behaviour analysis fully usable with no
calibration at all, and keeps NumPy out of the per-frame behaviour path.

### The V0.3.0 seam held

`GroundPlaneProjector` implements the *existing* `MotionProjector` protocol:

```python
class MotionProjector(Protocol):
    @property
    def space(self) -> MotionSpace: ...
    def project(self, point: Point) -> MotionPoint: ...
```

**No change was needed to `kinematics.py`, `history.py` or `models.py`.** Speed
becomes `m/s` and acceleration `m/s²` because `BehaviorSignal` derives its unit
from the coordinate space rather than hard-coding one. Heading stays `rad/s`,
being space-independent, and stationary duration stays seconds against a
threshold now expressed in metres per second.

## Venue coordinate system

Venue coordinates are metres in a **venue-local frame shared by every camera**,
not a per-camera metric frame. The operator picks one origin and one axis
orientation for the venue, then expresses each camera's reference points in it.

Nothing assumes a venue size, shape, camera count or coverage area. A camera
covering a doorway and one covering a festival field differ only in the numbers
their correspondences contain. Coverage regions need not be rectangular, need
not be equal, and may overlap. Venue coordinates may be negative — the origin
is wherever the operator put it.

`GroundPlaneCalibration.venue_id` records which frame was used, so two
calibrations can be **checked** for compatibility rather than assumed to agree.
`surface_id` names the planar surface (see the planar assumption below).

## Calibration correspondences

Each correspondence is one *"this pixel is that spot on the floor"* pair:

```text
image point (pixels, in the reference frame)  →  venue point (x metres, y metres)
```

- **Four** is the mathematical minimum: a homography has eight degrees of
  freedom and each point contributes two equations.
- **More than four is supported and preferred.** Extra points are fed into a
  least-squares fit, not discarded.
- Points are supplied manually. There is no automatic point discovery.

> **Why more than four matters.** With exactly four points the fit is
> exactly determined, so it reproduces them perfectly **however badly they were
> measured** — a reported reprojection error of zero tells you nothing. Only
> redundant points can contradict each other and expose a mistake. This is
> asserted directly in `test_four_noisy_points_still_report_zero_error`.

## Homography

Estimated with the **normalized Direct Linear Transform**:

1. **Hartley normalization** of both point sets — centroid to origin, mean
   distance `√2`. Without it, pixel values near 1000 and metre values near 10
   produce a badly conditioned system.
2. Each correspondence contributes two rows to a `2N × 9` matrix.
3. **SVD**; the solution is the right singular vector of the smallest singular
   value. Exact at four points, least-squares beyond.
4. Denormalize, then scale to canonical `H[2][2] == 1`.

### Why the maths is owned rather than delegated

OpenCV is already a dependency and `cv2.findHomography` would work. It is not
used, for the same reason point-in-polygon is ~20 lines of ray casting rather
than a geometry library: **the contract matters more than the implementation.**
Owning it means the degeneracy rules, the orientation convention and the failure
modes are ours to define and pin down with tests. It also keeps the entire
calibration package free of OpenCV.

### Orientation convention

`H` and `−H` describe the same projective transform. The estimator resolves the
ambiguity by orienting the result so the calibration's own points have positive
homogeneous `w`. This turns the sign of `w` into information: afterwards,
`w ≤ 0` means a point is **at or beyond the horizon** of the calibrated
surface — a real condition, not a numerical artefact.

Per-point projection uses a scalar path (nine multiplications, no NumPy),
because it runs once per tracked person per frame.

## Reference resolution handling

A calibration records the `ReferenceFrame` its pixel coordinates were authored
against. At runtime, `GroundPlaneProjector` folds the runtime-to-reference
rescale directly into the homography **once, at construction**:

```text
H_effective = H_reference @ diag(ref_w / runtime_w, ref_h / runtime_h, 1)
```

So a calibration authored at 1920×1080 gives identical venue coordinates when
the same video is decoded at 960×540, 640×360, 480×270 or 3840×2160 — all
tested. `test_ignoring_the_rescale_would_be_badly_wrong` shows the alternative
is metres of error.

**Aspect-ratio changes warn.** A pure rescale preserves the ratio; a change to
it means the frame was cropped, padded or stretched, so the same pixel no longer
shows the same part of the scene and no rescale can recover that. It warns
rather than rejects, because a deliberately anamorphic encode is legitimate and
the operator may know it is fine.

## Calibration quality

`CalibrationQuality` exposes both units:

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `mean_venue_error_m` | **Primary metric.** Mean reprojection error in metres |
| `max_venue_error_m` | Worst single point, in metres |
| `mean_image_error_px` | The same disagreement in reference-frame pixels |
| `max_image_error_px` | Worst single point, in pixels |
| `venue_extent_m` | Diagonal of the calibrated region |
| `relative_error` | `mean_venue_error_m / venue_extent_m` — scale-free |

**Venue-space metres is the primary metric** because it states the consequence
directly: "positions from this camera are about this far off". Pixel error is
reported alongside as the classical diagnostic, but it is resolution-dependent
and cannot be compared between cameras.

> **Reprojection error measures self-consistency, not accuracy.** It says how
> well the fit reproduces the operator's own points. If those points were
> measured wrongly on the floor, a perfect error simply means the calibration
> faithfully reproduces the wrong answer. Nothing here can detect that.

There are deliberately **no "good"/"bad" bands and no acceptance threshold.**
What counts as acceptable depends on the camera, the venue and the use, and
inventing a cutoff would dress an engineering judgement as a measured fact.

The one limit that exists — `DEFAULT_MAX_RELATIVE_REPROJECTION_ERROR = 0.10` —
is a **development safeguard against a broken fit**, not a quality bar. It is
expressed as a fraction of the calibrated region's own diagonal precisely so it
encodes no venue size: 10% of a 3 m doorway and 10% of a 60 m field both mean
"this is not a working calibration".

## Validation and failure

Every failure is a typed `CalibrationError` (or `ProjectionError`, its
subclass). Rejected: fewer than four correspondences, mismatched counts, wrong
array shapes, non-finite values, duplicate points, collinear layouts (any three
of four; wholly collinear for larger sets), degenerate configurations detected
by SVD conditioning, singular transforms, points straddling the horizon, and
fits that fail the reprojection safeguard.

**There is no fallback to normalized coordinates when a metric calibration is
invalid.** Falling back would silently change every downstream signal's unit
from metres to frame fractions — far worse than stopping. Loading solves the
homography eagerly, so a broken calibration fails at startup rather than a
thousand frames into a run.

`ProjectionError` is the one narrow exception, and it is about a *point*, not a
calibration: an anchor at or beyond the horizon has no ground position. The
`BehaviorAnalyzer` catches this specific type, skips that track for that frame,
counts it in `unprojectable_observations`, and continues — one impossible
detection must not end a run of thousands of frames. No signal is invented for
the skipped point.

## Planar assumption

**One homography describes one plane.** Every reference point, and every person
measured, must be on the same approximately flat floor.

Known unsupported cases:

- stairs and steps
- balconies and mezzanines
- raised platforms and stages
- multiple storeys
- strongly non-planar or sloped terrain

A person on a step is measured as though standing on the floor behind it, and
the error grows with the height difference. **V0.3.1 does not attempt to solve
this.** `surface_id` exists so a future milestone can hold several calibrated
surfaces per camera without redefining the config format; V0.3.1 supports
exactly one.

## Configuration format

```json
{
  "camera_id": "cam_01",
  "venue_id": "example_venue",
  "surface_id": "ground",
  "description": "SYNTHETIC EXAMPLE ONLY - ...",
  "reference_frame": { "width": 1920, "height": 1080 },
  "correspondences": [
    { "image": [512.0, 1010.0], "venue": [0.0, 0.0] },
    { "image": [1422.0, 1010.0], "venue": [6.0, 0.0] },
    { "image": [1235.0, 604.0], "venue": [6.0, 9.0] },
    { "image": [699.0, 604.0], "venue": [0.0, 9.0] },
    { "image": [967.0, 754.5], "venue": [3.0, 4.5] },
    { "image": [811.7, 696.4], "venue": [1.5, 6.0] }
  ]
}
```

Shipped as `config/calibration.example.json`. Zone configuration is untouched
and independent; a future venue config may combine cameras, calibration, zones
and topology, but V0.3.1 does not redesign configuration.

### Behaviour thresholds follow the space

`BehaviorAnalysisConfig` distance thresholds are in the **active coordinate
space**. Normalized defaults are meaningless under a metric projector: `0.02`
frame-widths/s is a plausible standing-still threshold, `0.02` m/s is 2 cm/s and
nobody would ever exceed it.

`BehaviorAnalysisConfig.for_ground_plane_metres()` supplies metric development
defaults — `stationary_speed_threshold = 0.20 m/s`,
`min_heading_displacement = 0.10 m` — keeping the same relationship the
normalized pair has. **The V0.3.0 normalized defaults are unchanged.**

Because mixing the two is silent and severe, `intended_space` lets a config
declare which space it was written for, and the analyzer refuses a mismatched
projector. It defaults to `None`, so every V0.3.0 configuration behaves exactly
as before.

## Multi-camera spatial architecture

`TestMultiCamera` runs two synthetic cameras with **entirely different image
geometry** (mixed axes, different focal lengths, different perspective) at
**different resolutions** (1920×1080 and 1280×720), calibrated against **one
venue frame**, observing one physical trajectory:

| | Camera A (1920×1080) | Camera B (1280×720) |
|---|---|---|
| True physical speed | `1.4 m/s` | `1.4 m/s` |
| Reported speed | `1.400000000000 m/s` | `1.400000000000 m/s` |

The two cameras' reported speeds differ by **4.4 × 10⁻¹⁵ m/s**.

The two cameras agree to floating-point precision on speed, acceleration,
stationary duration and venue position, sharing no pixel geometry whatsoever.

### Identity boundary — this is not association

**Spatial agreement is a property of the coordinate system, not evidence of
identity.** Two cameras reporting a person at `(12.4, 7.1)` and `(12.6, 7.0)`
are two independent observations that happen to be near each other. V0.3.1 draws
no conclusion from that.

The multi-camera test deliberately gives **the same numeric track id (`1`) on
both cameras** and asserts they remain two distinct subjects:

```python
SubjectRef("cam_a", 1) != SubjectRef("cam_b", 1)
```

Neither analyzer knows anything about the other's subject. **A camera-local
track ID is not a unique venue person.**

Not implemented, by design: re-identification, appearance embeddings, facial
recognition, biometric identifiers, persistent identity, cross-camera
association, venue-person IDs.

Spatial proximity may *later* become one input to an association layer. That
layer would sit above camera-local tracking and below venue-level intelligence,
and V0.3.1 deliberately stops short of it.

## Synthetic validation approach

All validation is against **synthetic geometry with known analytic answers**.
Image points are generated by pushing known venue points through explicit
venue-to-image homographies — a forward camera model — written in plain
arithmetic that shares no implementation with the code under test. If the
production maths were wrong, the oracle would not be wrong in the same way.

**No metric claim is made about any real footage.** We do not have trustworthy
measured venue reference coordinates for the V0.2.1 validation clips, so no
calibration is asserted against them and no metre distance for them is quoted
anywhere. `config/calibration.example.json` is labelled SYNTHETIC / EXAMPLE ONLY
in the file itself, and a test asserts that label is present.

## Known limitations

1. **Planar ground only.** Stairs, balconies, platforms and multiple floors are
   not supported and are silently mismeasured.
2. **No real-venue validation.** Every number is synthetic. Accuracy against
   real measured floors is unknown.
3. **Reprojection error is self-consistency, not accuracy.** Badly measured
   reference points produce a confident, wrong calibration.
4. **Four-point calibrations cannot report their own error** — they always fit
   exactly. Use more points.
5. **Manual calibration only.** No automatic point discovery, no camera pose
   estimation, no SLAM.
6. **A track ID is still not a person.** Inherited unchanged from V0.2.1 and
   V0.3.0; calibration does nothing to fix identity.
7. **Anchor error becomes metric error.** The bottom-centre anchor assumes feet
   are visible and correctly boxed; a truncated or jittering box now produces a
   wrong *position in metres*, and the error grows with distance from the camera
   as each pixel covers more ground.
8. **Accuracy degrades away from the calibrated region.** Extrapolation works
   mathematically but amplifies error, especially towards the horizon.
9. **Fixed camera assumed.** Any pan, tilt or zoom invalidates the calibration
   silently; there is no detection of camera movement.
10. **Metric behaviour thresholds are unvalidated development defaults**, like
    every other threshold in V0.3.
11. **No CLI exposure.** Calibration cannot yet be selected from the command
    line; it is constructed programmatically.
12. **Lens distortion is not modelled.** A wide-angle or fisheye camera violates
    the pinhole assumption a homography rests on, and no undistortion step
    exists.

## Verification

1008 tests pass (796 before this milestone, 212 added), `ruff check`,
`ruff format --check` and strict `mypy` all clean.

The load-bearing tests:

- **Perspective** — equal physical displacement at two depths; 2.116× image
  disagreement, 1.000× after calibration.
- **Multi-camera** — two geometries, two resolutions, one venue frame, agreeing
  to 4.4 × 10⁻¹⁵ m/s, with subjects staying separate under an identical track id.
- **Resolution independence** — five runtime resolutions against one
  calibration.
- **Exact recovery** — identity, affine and two perspective transforms recovered
  to ~1e-9, including at points never used for calibration.
- **Overdetermined fitting** — extra points used, not discarded; more points
  reduce the influence of one bad measurement.
- **Degeneracy** — collinear, coincident, duplicate, non-finite, mismatched,
  singular and horizon-straddling inputs all rejected with typed errors.
- **Regression** — all 796 prior tests unchanged; V0.2.1 defaults asserted
  untouched; normalized behaviour asserted unchanged.

## What V0.3.2 builds on top

Recommended scope, in priority order:

1. **Real-venue calibration and validation.** Measure reference points on an
   actual floor, then quantify accuracy against known distances. Until this
   happens, every metric claim rests on synthetic geometry.
2. **CLI and pipeline exposure** — `--calibration`, wiring through the
   composition root, and a signal output stream carrying venue coordinates.
3. **Venue-level configuration** combining cameras, calibrations and zones,
   plus ground-plane zones defined in metres rather than frame fractions.
4. **Calibration diagnostics** — draw the venue grid back onto a frame so an
   operator can see whether the calibration lines up with the floor.
5. **Camera-movement detection**, so a nudged camera is flagged rather than
   silently producing wrong metres.

Still out of scope: ReID, cross-camera association, venue-person identity,
automatic calibration, pose estimation, SLAM, 3D reconstruction, depth
estimation, aggression/intoxication/distress classification, interaction or
group signals, risk scoring, decision engines, LLM integration and operator
alerting.
