# V0.4.3 — Temporary Venue-Local Identity and Association Fusion

**Status:** implemented · **Engineering milestone:** V0.4.3 (V0.4.3a + V0.4.3b) ·
**Product generation:** V1 · **Not production ready**

## Purpose

V0.4.2 decides, at one venue instant, whether two camera-local tracklets correspond. It
names nobody: no `VenueSubjectId`, no registry, no merge exists anywhere in that milestone.
V0.4.3 is the pair of sub-milestones that turns a sustained, positive decision into a
*temporary, session-scoped hypothesis* that several camera-local tracklets are one person,
right now, in this venue — and gives that hypothesis its own namespace, its own lifecycle,
and its own conservative rules for growing or refusing to grow.

It is split deliberately in two:

- **V0.4.3a** builds the identity domain and its registry — the namespace, the membership
  shape, the provenance log, and the invariants a venue-local identity must never violate —
  with no knowledge of association at all.
- **V0.4.3b** builds the seam between them — the one function that reads a V0.4.2
  `AssociationRound` and applies safe, deterministic membership operations to a V0.4.3a
  `VenueSubjectRegistry`.

**It still does not identify a person.** A `VenueSubject` is a membership hypothesis, not a
name, a face, or a record that outlives the session. See
[What V0.4.3 does not do](#what-v043-does-not-do).

## Architectural position

```text
detection → camera-local tracking → calibration / spatial context
  → venue-time alignment / fan-in            (V0.4.0)
  → comparability gating + geometry          (V0.4.1)
  → deterministic association                (V0.4.2)
  → temporary venue-local identity           ← V0.4.3a builds this (identity/)
  → association → identity integration       ← V0.4.3b builds this (fusion/)
  → venue-level evidence → decision support  (later)
```

```text
services/vision/src/atreides_vision/
├── identity/                  # V0.4.3a — the domain and its registry
│   ├── models.py               #   TrackletKey, VenueSubjectId, TrackletMembership,
│   │                            #   MembershipEvent, VenueSubject
│   ├── config.py               #   VenueIdentityConfig
│   └── registry.py             #   VenueSubjectRegistry
└── fusion/                    # V0.4.3b — the seam
    ├── models.py               #   IntegrationOutcome, MembershipOutcome, IntegrationResult
    └── integration.py          #   integrate_round()
```

Layer boundaries are enforced by AST-walked import guards, not convention:

- `identity/` imports only `behavior.models.SubjectRef`, `timing.StreamRef` and `errors`. It
  does not import `association` at all and knows nothing of `AssociationRound`,
  `PairDecision`, or any V0.4.2 type — confirmed by `test_identity_boundaries.py`.
- `fusion/` is the *only* package permitted to import both `association` and `identity`
  (plus `errors`); no sibling signal layer, no model library, and nothing else in the tree
  is permitted to import `fusion` back — confirmed by `test_fusion_boundaries.py`.

Neither package absorbs the other's responsibility: `association` decides whether two
tracklets correspond; `identity` owns venue-local identity state and refuses to reconcile a
claim on its own; `fusion` holds the one policy that belongs to neither of them.

## V0.4.3a — the temporary venue-local identity domain

### `VenueSubjectId` semantics

`VenueSubjectId` (`identity/models.py`) is a frozen, ordered dataclass wrapping a single
`sequence: int ≥ 1`. It is:

- **allocated only by `VenueSubjectRegistry.create()`**, from a private, monotonically
  increasing counter starting at 1 — never a tracker id, never "lowest track id wins";
- **opaque** — its string form is `venue_subject_{sequence:06d}`; the sequence exists only
  to make allocation deterministic and ids orderable within one registry, not to carry
  meaning;
- **a distinct type and namespace from every camera-local track reference.** A
  `SubjectRef` (`camera_id, track_id`) is never reused as, or coerced into, a
  `VenueSubjectId`.

### `VenueSubjectRegistry` lifecycle and invariants

`VenueSubjectRegistry` (`identity/registry.py`) is the *only* place a `VenueSubjectId` is
allocated and the only place a `VenueSubject`'s membership changes. Every operation is
explicit — nothing here infers proximity, appearance, or any relationship between
tracklets; that decision belongs to a caller (V0.4.3b, or any future caller with equivalent
evidence).

Operations:

| Method | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| `create(membership)` | Allocates a new subject with one initial tracklet. Refuses if that tracklet is already claimed, or the registry is at `max_venue_subjects` capacity. |
| `add_membership(subject_id, membership)` | Adds a tracklet to an active subject. Refuses: unknown/retired subject; tracklet already claimed by this or another subject; venue time moving backwards relative to the subject's last activity; **a second active tracklet on a camera the subject already has one on**. |
| `remove_membership(subject_id, subject_ref, at_seconds)` | Removes an active tracklet. Does not retire the subject and does not destroy provenance — the removal is recorded, not erased. |
| `retire(subject_id, at_seconds)` | Ends the identity. A retired subject accepts no further membership changes; its tracklet claims are released so a later, different venue subject may claim the same tracklet. |
| `expire(now_seconds)` | Two deterministic passes, in canonical (sequence) order: retires subjects idle longer than `idle_expiry_seconds`, then purges subjects retired longer than `retention_seconds`. Purged subjects are no longer queryable. Driven only by venue time. |
| `get`, `subjects`, `active_subjects`, `owner_of`, `provenance_for` | Reads. All return in canonical order, never insertion or dict/set order. |

Invariants enforced at construction (`VenueSubject.__post_init__`) rather than by
convention:

- **members are always in canonical `tracklet_key` order**, never insertion order;
- **no duplicate tracklet** among a subject's active members;
- **at most one active member per camera** — the load-bearing invariant this milestone
  repeatedly relies on: a venue subject with two simultaneous tracklets on one camera would
  claim to be two places on that camera's view at once, and this layer never asserts that;
- `last_activity_seconds ≥ created_at_seconds`, and `retired_at_seconds`, when present,
  `≥ last_activity_seconds` — venue time only moves forward for one subject.

### Session-local, temporary, non-biometric

A `VenueSubjectRegistry` instance holds its entire state in three plain dicts
(`_subjects`, `_events`, `_claims`) plus a counter — no file, no database, no cross-session
or cross-venue linkage. Discarding the instance discards every identity it ever allocated.
`VenueIdentityConfig` (development defaults, each stating which direction it errs in when
wrong) gives "temporary" its numbers:

| Parameter | Default | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `idle_expiry_seconds` | 30.0 s | venue time an active subject may go without a membership change before `expire()` retires it |
| `retention_seconds` | 60.0 s | venue time a retired subject's record is kept before `expire()` purges it entirely |
| `max_venue_subjects` | 4096 | hard ceiling on subjects held at once (active + retired-not-yet-purged); `create()` refuses rather than silently evicting an active one |

Nothing stored is biometric. A `TrackletMembership` (`subject: SubjectRef, stream:
StreamRef, joined_at_seconds: float`) is nothing more than *which* camera-local tracklet
contributed and *when* — no appearance, no face, no template, and `MembershipEvent`
(`membership, action: ADDED | REMOVED, at_seconds`) records the same for a change, so a
removal is auditable rather than silently forgotten. Single-camera venues take exactly the
same path as multi-camera ones: one membership, one camera claimed, the same invariants —
there is no single-camera mode.

### Membership and provenance

`TrackletKey = tuple[str, int, int]` — `(camera_id, stream_epoch, track_id)` — names one
camera-local tracklet across a session; `tracklet_key(subject, stream)` builds it and
raises `IdentityError` if `subject` and `stream` disagree about which camera they describe.
`registry.provenance_for(subject_id)` returns every `MembershipEvent` ever recorded for a
subject, in chronological order by construction (each append is checked against
non-decreasing venue time), so a venue-local subject always resolves back to its
contributing `(camera, stream_epoch, track, joined_at_seconds)` observations — additive,
never lossy.

## V0.4.3b — the fusion/integration layer

### What it is

`integrate_round(registry, round_) -> IntegrationResult` (`fusion/integration.py`) is the
one function in this milestone. It applies one V0.4.2 `AssociationRound`'s decisions to a
V0.4.3a `VenueSubjectRegistry`, in the round's own canonical decision order, and returns a
complete, ordered account of what happened to every decision — including the ones that were
never eligible to change anything.

### Only `ASSOCIATED` may mutate identity state

`AssociationOutcome.ASSOCIATED` is the only outcome ever attempted against the registry.
`AMBIGUOUS`, `UNASSOCIATED` and `NOT_COMPARABLE` are always recorded `IGNORED`, with a
`detail` naming the outcome that made them ineligible — "no association here" from V0.4.2
never becomes a membership change here, whatever the reason for it.

### The exact `AssociationRound` → identity transition rules

For each `ASSOCIATED` decision, both tracklets' current ownership is looked up via
`registry.owner_of(tracklet_key(subject, stream))`, and exactly one of five outcomes
follows:

1. **Neither tracklet owned → `APPLIED` (create).** `registry.create()` allocates a new
   subject with the first tracklet, then `add_membership()` adds the second. The new
   subject holds both.
2. **One owned, one unowned → `APPLIED` (extend).** The unowned tracklet is added to the
   existing subject via `add_membership()` — the ordinary way an identity grows across a
   third, fourth, … camera.
3. **Both owned by the same subject → `NO_OP`.** Nothing changes. This is what makes
   reprocessing one `AssociationRound` idempotent: a decision already applied changes
   nothing the second time.
4. **Both owned by two different subjects → `REFUSED`.** Never merged. `subject_ids`
   reports both, in canonical (sequence) order — `tuple(sorted((owner_first,
   owner_second)))` — independent of which observation happened to own which subject.
5. **Camera-membership conflict → `REFUSED`.** A fifth case at the registry boundary: under
   case 2, if the subject that would be extended already has an active member on the new
   tracklet's camera, the addition is refused before `add_membership()` is even called (a
   proactive `VenueSubject.member_on()` check), rather than letting the registry raise
   `IdentityError` mid-round. The registry's own invariant — at most one active tracklet
   per camera per subject — never has to be violated to discover this.

Any other `IdentityError` the registry can raise (capacity exceeded, venue time moving
backwards relative to a subject's last activity) is not modeled as a sixth kind of refusal
and is left to propagate — a precondition violation belonging to the caller (retire or
expire subjects; process rounds in non-decreasing venue time), not an ordinary outcome of a
well-formed round.

### Why an existing pair of `VenueSubjectId`s is never automatically merged

Case 4 is the deliberate edge of this milestone's authority. Reconciling two already-formed
venue identities from one pairwise association would require deciding which subject's
provenance survives, whether the other's history is folded in or discarded, and what
confidence threshold justifies overriding two prior, independently-earned hypotheses — a
policy this milestone has no approved basis for inventing. Below the evidence for a full
merge, the correct output is no merge, reported rather than swallowed. Resolving genuine
cross-subject conflicts is a distinct, unbuilt capability and a product decision, not
something to improvise inside the integration seam.

### Failure/refusal philosophy

A wrong merge corrupts every venue-level number built on top of it and is harder to detect
than a missed one. Every rule above therefore chooses the missed association over the
guessed one: `AMBIGUOUS`/`UNASSOCIATED`/`NOT_COMPARABLE` stay non-mutating by construction,
a cross-subject conflict is refused whole rather than resolved by a heuristic (lowest
sequence wins, most recent activity wins, or any other tie-break), and a same-camera
conflict is refused rather than silently displacing the existing member. `REFUSED` and
`IGNORED` are first-class, reported outcomes — never a silently dropped decision.

### Idempotency

Calling `integrate_round` twice with the same round is safe: every decision that already
changed the registry resolves to `NO_OP` the second time (case 3), and every `REFUSED` or
`IGNORED` decision left the registry untouched to begin with, so reprocessing changes
nothing further. Pinned by a test that integrates a three-camera round twice and asserts
the second pass applies nothing.

### Deterministic ordering

`integrate_round` processes `round_.decisions` in their already-canonical `CandidateKey`
order (established by V0.4.2), so the result never depends on how the round's cameras were
enumerated when observations were collected. For a three-camera group, canonical order is
(A,B), (A,C), (B,C): the first decision creates a subject, the second extends it with the
third camera, and the third — both members already of the same subject — resolves to
`NO_OP`, not a third `APPLIED`. Pinned by a test sweeping every permutation of a
three-camera enumeration and asserting identical `applied`/`no_op` counts and identical
final `camera_ids` each time.

### Provenance preservation

Every `MembershipOutcome` carries the full originating `PairDecision` — its whole
`PairAssessment`, `SupportSummary` and `DecisionConfidence` — unmodified, so a caller can
see exactly why a membership was accepted, refused, or ignored without this layer inventing
a second copy of that evidence. Combined with `registry.provenance_for()`, a venue subject
resolves back to its contributing `(camera, stream_epoch, track)` observations with the
association evidence that joined them, at every step.

### The `TrackletKey` seam between association and identity

`association.models.TrackletKey` and `identity.models.TrackletKey` are independently
declared, structurally identical aliases for `tuple[str, int, int]` — deliberately so, per
each package's own boundary guard, which forbids either from importing the other.
`fusion/integration.py` is the one place both meanings are in scope, and it never treats
either alias as a value to pass across the seam: every registry lookup is built fresh from
a `TrackObservation`'s own `subject` (`SubjectRef`) and `stream` (`StreamRef`) through
`identity.tracklet_key()` — the shared vocabulary both packages already key their own
aliases from. No raw tuple crosses the boundary, and no third alias is introduced.

## Single-camera behavior

With one camera, no observation ever has a counterpart to pair with, so V0.4.2 emits no
`ASSOCIATED` decision and `integrate_round` has nothing to apply — the registry stays
empty. This is the same path as the multi-camera case taking zero eligible decisions, not a
special mode, an error, or a degenerate crash.

## Configuration and lifecycle assumptions

- `VenueIdentityConfig` values (`idle_expiry_seconds=30.0`, `retention_seconds=60.0`,
  `max_venue_subjects=4096`) are **development defaults, unmeasured against any real
  deployment** — inherited unchanged from V0.4.3a; V0.4.3b introduces no configuration of
  its own.
- `integrate_round` assumes the registry it is given belongs to one venue session and that
  `expire()` is called by the caller on some cadence; V0.4.3b does not call `expire()`
  itself and does not decide when a subject goes idle.
- Venue time is assumed non-decreasing across successive calls, exactly as V0.4.0–V0.4.2
  already require; nothing in this milestone changes that requirement.

## Validation

**Synthetic only.** Every behaviour in both sub-milestones is established by unit tests
over hand-built observations, decisions and registry states with analytically known
answers. This proves the rules; it says nothing about occlusion, crowding, real multi-camera
footage or a real venue.

- Human-verified ground truth: **`NOT_ANNOTATED`.** No cross-camera identity has been
  checked against a human annotation.
- Proxy/system metrics: **none emitted.** No duplicate-count figure, no merge rate, no
  false-merge rate is produced by any pipeline, because no pipeline runs this code.

## Known limitations

- **No real-world validation whatsoever.** Every threshold and every rule above is a
  development default or an unmeasured design choice; nothing here has met a real
  occlusion, a real crowd, or two real cameras that overlap.
- **Case 4 (cross-subject conflict) has no resolution path at all**, automatic or
  operator-assisted. A venue that produces two independently-created subjects for one real
  person — for example through a missed early association — has no mechanism in this
  milestone to reconcile them; the duplicate persists until one subject expires.
- **No duplicate-occupancy suppression at venue level.** A `VenueSubject` existing does not
  yet feed any headcount or unique-visitor figure; that consumption is unbuilt.
- **`expire()` is caller-driven, not automatic.** `integrate_round` never calls it; a caller
  that never calls `expire()` accumulates idle and retired subjects up to
  `max_venue_subjects`, at which point `create()` starts refusing.
- **Confidence carried through, not re-evaluated.** `MembershipOutcome.decision.confidence`
  is V0.4.2's `PROVISIONAL`/`CORROBORATED` value, unchanged; this milestone adds no
  identity-level confidence measure of its own (e.g. no notion of "how many cameras agree"
  beyond the count of active members).
- **Same-camera refusal (case 5) is checked once, at the moment of extension.** It does not
  reconcile a subject that somehow already violates the invariant (which the registry's own
  construction-time check makes structurally impossible, but is worth stating as a design
  assumption rather than a runtime guarantee re-verified by this layer).

## What V0.4.3 does not do

- **No automatic merge or split of venue identities.** Case 4 is reported, never resolved.
- **No duplicate-occupancy suppression, no venue-level headcount or unique-visitor figure.**
- **No venue-level interaction or crowd fusion.** Those layers remain camera-local.
- **No appearance embeddings, no ReID, no colour histogram, no crop, no biometric, no face.**
- **No long-gap or non-overlap hand-off matching.** Only V0.4.2's simultaneous, sustained
  associations ever reach the registry.
- **No cross-session or cross-venue identity**, and no durable identity store of any kind.
- **No pipeline wiring, no CLI command, no JSONL schema, no `schema_version`.** Nothing in
  a running session calls `integrate_round`.
- **No Risk / Decision Engine and no decision-support output.**
- **No real-world validation claim, and no production-readiness claim.**

## Next architectural seam

The next seam is wiring `identity`/`fusion` output into venue-level evidence and
decision-support: consuming `VenueSubjectRegistry` state (and, eventually, duplicate
suppression built on top of it) to produce the aggregated, cross-camera evidence that a
Risk / Decision Engine can reason over — plus, as a distinct and separately-approved piece
of work, a confidence-aware policy for the case-4 cross-subject conflict this milestone
deliberately leaves unresolved. Neither is built here.
