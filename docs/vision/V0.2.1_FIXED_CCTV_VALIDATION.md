# V0.2.1 — Fixed-CCTV Validation

**Status:** complete · **Verdict:** PASS WITH DOCUMENTED LIMITATIONS

## Purpose

V0.1 delivered SEE + TRACK and V0.2 added zones and crowd metrics, but both ran
on defaults that had never been tested against real fixed-camera footage. V0.2.1
is not a feature release. Its only job was to answer one question:

> Which detector/tracker configuration should a standard Atreides run use, and
> what do we actually know about how it behaves?

The answer was reached by measurement on three materially different fixed
cameras, and the winning configuration is now the default. Everything below is
what the evidence supports — and, just as importantly, what it does not.

## Final validated configuration

| Component | Setting | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Detector | RF-DETR Nano confidence | **0.30** |
| Tracker | implementation | **OC-SORT** |
| OC-SORT | `high_conf_det_threshold` | **0.30** |
| OC-SORT | `minimum_iou_threshold` | 0.30 |
| OC-SORT | `lost_track_buffer` | 30 frames |
| OC-SORT | `minimum_consecutive_frames` | 2 |
| OC-SORT | `direction_consistency_weight` | 0.2 |
| OC-SORT | `delta_t` | 3 |
| Deduplication | Rule B3 | **on by default** |

A run with no tuning flags resolves to exactly this. The startup banner prints
`Profile: V0.2.1 validated defaults`, or `CUSTOM` if anything was overridden.

These are **validated development defaults**, not a claim of production-wide
optimality. They were chosen because they are the best-evidenced option across
three proxy scenes, not because they are proven correct for any particular venue.

## Why OC-SORT replaced ByteTrack as the default

ByteTrack fragmented identities through short occlusions. Diagnostic
instrumentation traced a representative failure: a track was alive for 130
frames, lost its detection during an occlusion, and its Kalman prediction
drifted ~189 px while the person was hidden. When the person reappeared, the
best available IoU against the predicted box was 0.22 — below the 0.30
association gate — and 0.0 against the last observation, so both association
stages failed and a new id was opened.

OC-SORT scores association on direction consistency as well as IoU, and
re-associates against the last *observation* rather than only the drifting
prediction. On the corridor scene this held ids through 3–4× bounding-box scale
change over 40–50 seconds, which is the case ByteTrack handled worst.

ByteTrack remains fully supported: `--tracker bytetrack`. Note that its
`track_activation_threshold` is still 0.50, so pairing it with the 0.30 detector
default means detections between 0.30 and 0.50 cannot start a ByteTrack track.
That is ByteTrack's own baseline, deliberately left untouched by V0.2.1.

## Why detector confidence moved 0.50 → 0.30

A threshold sweep at 0.50 / 0.40 / 0.30 on the dense scene showed 0.50 was
discarding large numbers of genuine mid- and far-field people. The additional
detections admitted at 0.30 were inspected rather than assumed: on the night
scene, all 24 of the lowest-confidence detections — every one sitting exactly on
the 0.30 floor — were real pedestrians, not noise. No false-positive problem
appeared at 0.30 in any scene: box geometry stayed person-shaped (median aspect
ratio ~2.8, under 1% wider than tall) and no static-background artefacts were found.

The cost is that weak detections are duplicate-prone: the detector sometimes
emits two near-identical boxes on one person, each capable of seeding its own
track. That is what Rule B3 exists to clean up.

## Rule B3 — geometric detection deduplication

Two detections in the **same frame** are treated as one person when **all three**
hold:

| Axis | Threshold |
|---|---|
| IoU | ≥ 0.85 |
| area similarity — `min(area) / max(area)` | ≥ 0.95 |
| normalised centre distance — centre separation ÷ mean box diagonal | ≤ 0.03 |

Detections satisfying this pairwise are grouped into connected components; each
group keeps its **highest-confidence** member, ties broken by earliest input
position so the result is deterministic.

Scope is deliberately narrow. It sees only the current frame's anonymous box
geometry: **no track ids, no appearance descriptors, no re-identification, no
biometrics, no temporal state of any kind.** The function is pure and stateless,
and detector-agnostic.

**How the thresholds were chosen.** An earlier variant (B0: IoU ≥ 0.90, centre
≤ 0.02) missed duplicates that were plainly visible. A cross-clip study of five
candidate policies found that relaxing centre distance alone changed *nothing* —
at IoU ≥ 0.90 with area similarity ≥ 0.95, observed pairs never exceeded 0.016
centre distance, so that gate was never binding. IoU was the binding term. B3
relaxes IoU to 0.85, which added 7 removals across two clips; all 7 were
inspected at full resolution and confirmed to be two boxes on one person.

Centre distance stays tight on purpose: it is the term that keeps genuinely
adjacent pedestrians apart. Confirmed two-person overlaps sit at centre distance
0.112 and above — more than 3.7× outside the gate.

Disable with `--no-deduplicate-detections`.

## Validation scenes

| # | Clip | Resolution / FPS | Geometry |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `dense_venue_04.mp4` | 1920×1080 / 30 | Elevated fixed camera, dense daylight street, lateral pedestrian flow |
| 2 | `low_light_venue_02.mp4` | 1920×1080 / 30 | Fixed night street under strong artificial lighting |
| 3 | `venue_entrance_02.mp4` | 384×288 / 25 | Head-on corridor, depth-axis motion, strong scale change, crossings |

Scene 2 is **night with substantial artificial lighting**, not a genuinely dark
or noisy interior. It should not be cited as low-light robustness evidence.

## Main evidence

| | Scene 1 (dense) | Scene 2 (night) | Scene 3 (corridor) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Frames | 1500 | 1050 | 1590 |
| Raw detections | 34,750 | 19,232 | 12,691 |
| Removed by B3 | 29 (0.083%) | 25 (0.130%) | 17 (0.134%) |
| Mean reported tracks/frame | 21.61 | 17.24 | 7.84 |
| Unique track ids | 240 | 126 | 45 |
| Reported / tracker-input ratio | 0.9336 | 0.9425 | 0.9832 |
| Median track lifetime (frames) | 68 | 61.5 | 118 |
| Ids ≤ 5 frames | 17.1% | 18.3% | 24.4% |

Findings that carried across scenes:

- **B3 stayed conservative everywhere** — between 0.08% and 0.13% of detections.
  Every removal that was manually inspected, across all three scenes, was two
  boxes on one person. **No manually verified removal collapsed two genuine
  people.** On scene 3 all 17 removals were inspected: 17/17 same-person, 0
  collapses.
- **Coverage was never harmed.** Enabling B3 left mean/median/max reported
  tracks per frame unchanged and slightly *raised* the reported/detection ratio.
- **Duplicate-id behaviour improved** where it was worst: on scene 2, seven
  track-id pairs that overlapped at IoU ≥ 0.5 disappeared, and IoU ≥ 0.7
  overlaps fell 7 → 5.
- **Identity survived severe scale change.** On scene 3, ids were retained
  through 3.26× growth over 42 s (id 2, 44.6 → 145.4 px, approaching) and 3.82×
  over 52 s (id 7, receding). No fragmentation was attributable to scale change.
- **No ID swap between two distinct people was observed anywhere in scene 3.**
- **25 FPS caused no observable problem.** `lost_track_buffer = 30` frames is
  1.20 s at 25 FPS versus 1.00 s at 30 FPS. The longest coasting gap actually
  recovered was 20 frames (0.80 s) and no gap exceeded 30 frames, so the extra
  budget was never exercised.

## Verdict: PASS WITH DOCUMENTED LIMITATIONS

The configuration held recall, held identity through severe scale change,
produced no observed identity swaps, and kept deduplication conservative across
three materially different fixed cameras. The residual failure mode is
same-person id multiplicity, described below.

## Limitations

These are load-bearing. Read them before relying on any number this pipeline emits.

1. **Same-person ID multiplicity remains possible.** One person can pick up a
   short-lived duplicate id, carry two concurrent ids for a few frames, or be
   assigned a new id after an occlusion. Observed on scene 3 in all three forms.
2. **Unique track ids are NOT equivalent to unique real people.** Because of (1),
   the unique-id count is an **upper bound** on distinct people, not a census.
   Any occupancy or footfall figure derived from unique ids will over-count.
3. **Degradation below roughly 40 px person height.** Detection confidence falls
   monotonically with distance — median 0.913 for people ≥ 105 px, 0.747 at
   ~41 px — and the id-multiplicity failures cluster on small, weak, slow-moving
   far-field subjects.
4. **The 10–20 px person regime is unvalidated.** No validation scene contained
   people that small in any quantity; scene 3's deepest band still yielded
   32–48 px people.
5. **Genuinely dark or noisy venue interiors are unvalidated.** Scene 2 was
   brightly lit night, and its detections were *more* confident than daylight
   scene 1's, which is the opposite of a hard low-light test.
6. **Coloured or strobing nightclub lighting is unvalidated.** No scene contained
   it. Strobing in particular may interact badly with a motion-model tracker.
7. **No appearance ReID or biometric matching is used.** Association is geometry
   and motion only. This is a deliberate design constraint, not an omission.
8. **Tracking identity is camera-local and session-local.** Track ids are
   ephemeral, meaningful only within one run of one camera, and are not persisted.
9. **Multi-camera fusion is future work.** There is no cross-camera identity today.
10. **The validation footage is proxy footage.** Public CCTV-style clips are not
    the venues Atreides targets. These results are evidence about the
    configuration, not proof of production venue performance.

**This is not a claim of production readiness.**

## Reproducing

```bash
# The validated defaults — no tuning flags needed.
uv run atreides-vision run \
  --input data/input/validation/dense_venue_04.mp4 \
  --output artifacts/tracked.mp4

# Pre-V0.2.1 behaviour, for comparison.
uv run atreides-vision run --input ... --output ... \
  --tracker bytetrack --confidence 0.50 --no-deduplicate-detections
```

Add `--tracking-debug-output artifacts/debug.jsonl` for per-frame tracker
internals. It is observational and does not change tracking behaviour.
