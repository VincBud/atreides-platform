# V0.3.0 — Behaviour Signal Foundation

**Status:** implemented · **Substep:** V0.3.0 of V0.3 · **Not production ready**

> **Superseded in part by V0.3.1.** The "current normalized-image limitation"
> below is lifted for runs that supply a ground-plane calibration — see
> [V0.3.1_GROUND_PLANE_CALIBRATION.md](V0.3.1_GROUND_PLANE_CALIBRATION.md). It
> still applies in full to the normalized projector, which remains the default.

## Purpose

V0.1 finds people and follows them. V0.2 says where activity happens. V0.3 asks
a different question:

> How is this person moving, right now, in a way a machine can measure and a
> human can check?

V0.3.0 is the foundation for that: it converts camera-local track observations
into **neutral, measurable temporal signals** — speed, acceleration, turn rate,
stationary duration — and nothing else. There is no interpretation layer, no
risk score, no classifier. A signal here is a number with a unit, a subject and
a timestamp. What it *means* is deliberately somebody else's problem, and that
somebody does not exist yet.

The milestone's real deliverable is not the four signals. It is the
**architecture that keeps them valid when the venue changes** — a different
room size, a different camera, a different resolution, eventually a calibrated
floor plan and multiple overlapping views.

## Architecture

```text
tracks ──▶ anchor ──▶ projection ──▶ history ──▶ kinematics ──▶ signals
           (bottom     (MotionSpace)  (bounded,   (pure         (typed,
            centre)                    segmented)  stateless)    unit-carrying)
```

```text
services/vision/src/atreides_vision/behavior/
├── __init__.py       # package boundary, scope and privacy statement
├── coordinates.py    # MotionSpace, MotionPoint, MotionProjector, NormalizedFrameProjector
├── models.py         # SubjectRef, BehaviorObservation, BehaviorSignal, BehaviorFrame
├── config.py         # BehaviorAnalysisConfig — every threshold, with units
├── history.py        # SubjectHistory, SubjectHistoryStore — bounded, segmented
├── kinematics.py     # pure formulas: speed, acceleration, heading, stationarity
└── analyzer.py       # BehaviorAnalyzer — the one thing the pipeline talks to
```

Each module has one job, and the dependency arrows only point one way:
`analyzer → history → models → coordinates`, with `kinematics` depending on
nothing but `coordinates` and a narrow structural protocol.

The layer imports **no detector, no tracker, and not the zone layer**. It
consumes `Track` objects and a `MotionProjector`. RF-DETR, OC-SORT and ByteTrack
remain replaceable without touching a line of it — the same property the
detector/tracker adapters exist to provide, extended to behaviour.

## The coordinate-space abstraction

### Why raw pixel velocity is insufficient

Every signal here is a distance over a time. The time is unambiguous — it comes
from the source frame rate. The distance is not.

A pixel means something different in every shot. The same person walking the
same speed produces:

- **5× more pixels per second** at 1920 wide than at 384 wide;
- **far more pixels per step** near the camera than at the back of the room;
- different values again on a camera mounted higher, angled differently, or
  fitted with a wider lens.

So a "speed" measured in pixels is not a property of the person. It is a
property of the person *and* the camera *and* the resolution *and* where they
happen to be standing. Any threshold built on it silently encodes one specific
installation, and breaks the moment a second camera exists. That is exactly the
kind of assumption this milestone was required not to bake in.

### What was built instead

No algorithm in the package ever sees a pixel. A `MotionProjector` converts a
person's image-space anchor into a `MotionPoint`, which **carries the space it
belongs to**:

```python
class MotionProjector(Protocol):
    @property
    def space(self) -> MotionSpace: ...
    def project(self, point: Point) -> MotionPoint: ...
```

`MotionPoint` refuses to be combined with a point from another space, so a
normalized value can never be silently subtracted from a metric one.

Two spaces are named. **One is implemented.**

| Space | Distance unit | Status |
|---|---|---|
| `NORMALIZED_IMAGE` | `normalized_frame_unit` | Implemented — `NormalizedFrameProjector` |
| `GROUND_PLANE_METRES` | `m` | **Reserved. Nothing produces it in V0.3.0** |

Units are derived from the space at signal-construction time, so the same code
reports `normalized_frame_unit/s` today and `m/s` the day a calibrated
projector is added — with no change to any formula.

### The person anchor

A person's position is the **bottom-centre of their bounding box**, roughly
where their feet are. This was already the zone layer's convention, so V0.3
adopted it rather than inventing a second one: both now call
`BoundingBox.bottom_center`, and a test asserts the two layers agree. The
anchor is injectable (`PersonAnchor`) for cameras where feet are the wrong
point — overhead or fisheye mounts — without touching the analyzer.

### Current limitation: normalized image space is camera-local

**This must be read before any signal from V0.3.0 is used for anything.**

`NormalizedFrameProjector` gives resolution independence and nothing more:

- ✅ The same scene at 384×288 and 1920×1080 produces **identical** signals.
  This is directly tested, at six resolutions, for every signal type.
- ✅ The same scene at 10 FPS and 50 FPS produces identical signals.
- ❌ Values are **not metres** and do not convert to metres.
- ❌ Values from **two arbitrary cameras are not comparable**. A person crossing
  a doorway camera scores far higher than the same person crossing a wide
  festival shot, because "one frame width" is a different physical distance in
  each.
- ❌ Values are **not comparable across the depth of a single frame**. Someone
  near the camera covers more of the frame per step than someone at the back.
- ❌ Zones do not fix this. A zone bounds *where* a measurement was taken, not
  what its distance unit means.

Treat every V0.3.0 number as **relative, per-camera, and only meaningful
against other measurements from the same camera at the same depth**.

### Future calibrated ground-plane path

The intended next step, deliberately not built here:

1. Per camera, an operator marks four floor reference points with known
   real-world spacing.
2. A homography maps image points onto floor-plane coordinates.
3. A `GroundPlaneProjector` implements the same `MotionProjector` protocol and
   returns `MotionPoint(..., space=GROUND_PLANE_METRES)`.

At that point speed reads in `m/s`, thresholds become physically meaningful,
and values become comparable across cameras and across the depth of a frame —
**with no change to `kinematics.py`, `history.py`, `models.py` or the
analyzer**. That claim is not a hope: a stub metric projector in the test suite
already drives the full analyzer and produces `m/s` signals, proving the seam
holds.

No calibration, homography or reference-point capture exists in V0.3.0.

## Identity boundaries

### Camera-local: a track ID is not a person

**`track_id` ≠ unique real-world person.** It is a handle on a box that one
tracker followed through one camera's view during one session. The same person
who walks out of frame and back in may return with a new id. This inherits
directly from V0.2.1 limitation (2), and V0.3.0 does nothing to fix it.

Every signal therefore carries a `SubjectRef` — the `(camera_id, track_id)`
pair — never a bare integer. Two cameras that both hand out track id `7` produce
two distinct subjects, with entirely separate history. That is tested at both
the store level and the analyzer level.

### Future multi-camera fusion

`SubjectRef` deliberately has **only two fields**. A venue-local identity column
was considered and rejected: it would sit permanently `None` and imply a fusion
capability that does not exist.

When fusion arrives it will sit **above camera-local tracking and below
venue-level intelligence**, mapping `SubjectRef` values onto venue subjects.
Because signals travel with a whole `SubjectRef` rather than a raw id, that
mapping can be introduced without changing any behaviour algorithm.

### Privacy

V0.3.0 is camera-local and non-biometric:

- No appearance embeddings.
- No facial or other biometric recognition.
- No persistent person identity — all state is in memory and discarded at exit.
- No cross-camera identity matching.
- No video or crop is retained; an observation keeps a projected point, a box
  and a timestamp.

Signals are derived from anonymous box geometry and elapsed time only.

## Implemented signals

Four signals. All neutral measurements. `{d}` is the active space's distance
unit — `normalized_frame_unit` in V0.3.0.

| Signal | Definition | Unit | Emitted when |
|---|---|---|---|
| `speed` | Path length over the window ÷ elapsed time | `{d}/s` | ≥2 samples spanning real time |
| `acceleration` | Split-half difference of mean speed ÷ time between half-centres | `{d}/s²` | ≥2 samples in each half |
| `heading_change_rate` | Wrapped difference of each half's net-travel direction ÷ time between half-centres | `rad/s` | both halves moved ≥ `min_heading_displacement` |
| `stationary_duration` | Continuous seconds with speed below `stationary_speed_threshold` | `s` | a stationary spell is in progress |

Design decisions worth stating:

- **Speed uses path length, not net displacement.** Someone dancing on the spot
  is moving; straight-line displacement over a window would report zero. This
  matches the zone layer's definition.
- **Rates of change use split-half differencing, not frame-to-frame deltas.**
  Differencing consecutive frames divides sensor noise by a tiny `dt` and yields
  jitter. Splitting the window, measuring each half and differencing across the
  gap between their centres is *exact* for constant acceleration — asserted in
  the tests, not approximated.
- **Heading is undefined below a displacement floor.** A standing person has no
  direction of travel. Reporting one derived from box jitter would manufacture
  a turn rate out of noise, so no heading signal is emitted at all.
- **`None` is never replaced by zero.** "Not enough history to say" and
  "measured as not moving" are different facts, and a signal that cannot be
  computed is simply omitted from the frame.
- **`quality`** reports how much of the configured window the estimate actually
  rests on, in `[0, 1]`. A freshly acquired track scores low. It is *not* a
  probability and says nothing about whether the value is correct.
- **`stationary_duration` outlives the retained window**, because a person can
  stand still far longer than the couple of seconds of history kept. It is
  carried as a spell-start timestamp, and any discontinuity resets it — we
  cannot claim someone stood still through an interval we could not see.

### What is explicitly not produced

No signal labels anyone as aggressive, intoxicated, suspicious, dangerous,
distressed, or anything else. `SignalType` has exactly four members and a test
pins that list. Those are interpretations; they require evidence this milestone
does not have, and they do not belong in a measurement layer.

## Motion segments and missing observations

A history is not "the last N observations" — it is the current **motion
segment**: the unbroken run in which no gap exceeded
`max_observation_gap_seconds`.

- **Short gap** (a dropped detection or two): bridged. The person kept moving
  and the samples either side are comparable.
- **Long gap**: starts a new segment and discards what came before. Where
  somebody went while unobserved is unknown, and averaging across the gap would
  invent a straight line through it — usually a slow one, since the elapsed time
  is large. Reporting nothing is more honest.

## Configuration

`BehaviorAnalysisConfig`, reachable as `VisionConfig.behavior`. Thresholds in
"distance units" are in the **active coordinate space** — so a configuration
tuned for normalized image motion is *not* valid for a metric projector.

| Setting | Default | Unit | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| `speed_window_seconds` | `1.0` | s | Window speed is averaged over |
| `acceleration_window_seconds` | `1.0` | s | Window differenced for acceleration |
| `heading_window_seconds` | `1.0` | s | Window differenced for turn rate |
| `min_heading_displacement` | `0.01` | `{d}` | Movement floor below which direction is undefined |
| `stationary_speed_threshold` | `0.02` | `{d}/s` | Speed below which a subject counts as stationary |
| `max_observation_gap_seconds` | `0.5` | s | Longest gap motion may be measured across |
| `history_window_seconds` | `2.0` | s | History retained per subject |
| `max_samples_per_subject` | `512` | count | Absolute per-subject sample cap |
| `stale_subject_ttl_frames` | `150` | frames | When an unseen subject's history is dropped |
| `max_tracked_subjects` | `1024` | count | Hard ceiling; least-recently-seen evicted |

Construction validates that `history_window_seconds` covers the longest signal
window, so a signal can never be silently computed over less history than
configured.

> **These are DEVELOPMENT defaults.** Unlike the V0.2.1 detector and tracker
> defaults, **no measurement backs them.** They were chosen to be reasonable and
> to make the signals testable. They are not validated real-world thresholds and
> are not safety thresholds. `stationary_speed_threshold` in particular is
> highly camera-dependent and is the first thing to re-tune per camera.

**No V0.2.1 validated default was changed.** Detector confidence `0.30`,
OC-SORT default, OC-SORT high-confidence `0.30`, Rule B3 on, and every other
validated tracking value are untouched — and a test asserts it.

## Pipeline integration

`VideoProcessor` gained one optional collaborator, `behavior_analyzer`, exactly
as it gained `zone_analyzer` in V0.2. When present it observes the same tracks
and records signals; when absent nothing happens.

The integration is **measurement-only**: signals are not drawn, not written to
any stream, and do not influence anything the pipeline outputs. A regression
test renders twenty frames with and without an analyzer attached and asserts the
output is **byte-identical**.

**There is no CLI flag yet.** Exposing behaviour analysis to operators needs an
output format and a display decision, which belong with V0.3.1 rather than being
half-built here. The processor seam exists and is tested; the composition root
does not use it yet.

Zone context is supported but not yet wired: `BehaviorAnalyzer.update()` accepts
an optional `zone_ids_by_track` mapping of opaque strings, which it copies onto
signals and never interprets. The processor does not populate it, which is why
behaviour analysis works identically with or without zones configured.

## Known limitations

1. **Normalized image space is camera-local.** Values are not metres, not
   comparable between cameras, and not comparable across the depth of one
   frame. See the coordinate-space section — this is the dominant limitation.
2. **A track ID is not a person.** Inherited unchanged from V0.2.1. Signals
   about a subject are signals about a *tracklet*; an occlusion that splits a
   person into two ids splits their behaviour history too, and a
   `stationary_duration` resets when it happens.
3. **Defaults are unvalidated.** No footage was measured to choose them.
4. **Fixed-camera assumption.** On panning or handheld footage the camera's own
   motion is measured as the subject's, exactly as with zone speed.
5. **No perspective compensation.** Someone walking towards the camera changes
   apparent speed purely through geometry.
6. **Turn rate is noisy for slow movement.** The displacement floor suppresses
   the worst of it by emitting nothing, at the cost of no signal for slow
   walkers.
7. **Signals are per-frame and unsmoothed.** No temporal aggregation, no
   baselining, no percentile over a session. That belongs to a consumer.
8. **Not exposed to operators.** No CLI flag, no output stream, no overlay.
9. **Boxes are noisy.** Detector jitter propagates into every signal, and at
   small box sizes the anchor moves by a meaningful fraction of a frame between
   frames without the person moving at all.

## Verification

796 tests pass (573 before this milestone, 223 added), `ruff check`,
`ruff format --check` and strict `mypy` all clean.

The load-bearing tests:

- **Resolution invariance** — identical signals at 384×288 and 1920×1080 for
  walking, accelerating and stationary trajectories, plus turning; and an
  explicit demonstration that a pixel-based speed would have differed 5×.
- **Analytic correctness** — constant velocity, constant acceleration and
  right-angle turns are recovered exactly from closed-form synthetic paths.
- **Frame-rate independence** — 10 FPS and 50 FPS agree.
- **State isolation** — two track ids, and two cameras sharing track id `7`, do
  not leak into each other.
- **Gaps** — short gaps bridged, long gaps produce no invented signal.
- **Memory bounds** — a thousand churning track ids stay bounded.
- **Determinism** — repeated runs produce identical records.
- **Regression** — attaching an analyzer changes no rendered pixel.

## Next substep — V0.3.1 (now implemented)

The recommended first item was **ground-plane calibration**, and it has since
been built: see
[V0.3.1_GROUND_PLANE_CALIBRATION.md](V0.3.1_GROUND_PLANE_CALIBRATION.md).

It confirmed the central architectural claim above. A `GroundPlaneProjector`
implementing the same `MotionProjector` protocol was added, and **no change was
required to `kinematics.py`, `history.py` or `models.py`** — speed simply
started arriving in `m/s`. Limitation (1) of this document is therefore lifted
*when a calibration is supplied*; it still applies in full to any run using the
normalized projector, which remains the default.

Two additions were made on the behaviour side, both additive and both leaving
V0.3.0 behaviour unchanged when unused:

- `BehaviorAnalysisConfig.for_ground_plane_metres()`, because the normalized
  distance thresholds are meaningless in metres, plus an optional
  `intended_space` declaration the analyzer checks against the projector.
- A narrow `ProjectionError` guard in the analyzer, because a calibrated
  projector has a horizon and an anchor beyond it has no ground position. The
  track is skipped for that frame rather than ending the run.

Still outstanding from the original list, and carried into V0.3.2:

1. **Signal output stream.** JSONL, sampled like the zone metrics stream, plus
   the `--behavior` / `--calibration` CLI flags and composition-root wiring.
2. **Zone context wiring.** Expose per-track zone membership from
   `ZoneAnalyzer` and pass it through the processor.
3. **Per-camera threshold tuning** against real footage, replacing development
   defaults with measured ones — now also including real-venue calibration
   measurement, which V0.3.1 could not do.

Deliberately still out of scope: risk scoring, aggression or intoxication
classification, ReID, multi-camera person association, appearance embeddings,
and any model training.
