# V0.3.5 — Spatial Cluster & Crowd Dynamics

**Status:** implemented · **Substep:** V0.3.5 of V0.3 · **Not production ready**

## Purpose

V0.3.0–V0.3.3 measured people one at a time. V0.3.4 measured them two at a time.
V0.3.5 measures the arrangement of *several* observed subjects at once: which of
them are spatially connected, how tightly packed that arrangement is, how
concentrated the surrounding neighbourhood is, whether the members are moving in
a common direction, and how long the same composition has held together.

**It measures. It does not interpret.** "Six connected subjects, RMS radius
1.9 m, mean local density 0.31 subjects/m², heading coherence 0.86, held for
12 s" is a measurement. Whether that is a queue, a dance floor, a smoking group,
a bottleneck or a crowd surge is a question this milestone deliberately cannot
answer.

There is no crowd risk score, density verdict, surge, crush, panic, unrest,
escalation, stampede or safety signal anywhere in this layer, and a spatial
cluster is **not** a social group, friendship group, affiliation or persistent
entity of any kind.

## Architecture

```text
tracks
  → BehaviorAnalyzer          (projection, per-subject history, individual signals)
  → BehaviorFrame             (snapshots: position + motion + zone labels)
  → SpatialClusterAnalyzer    (radius graph → components → cluster measures)
  → CrowdFrame
  → JsonlCrowdWriter          (separate versioned stream)
```

```text
services/vision/src/atreides_vision/crowd/
├── models.py      # ClusterMembers, SpatialClusterSnapshot, CrowdFrame
├── config.py      # radii and bounds, with normalized and metric factories
├── geometry.py    # pure spatial maths (centroid, spread, density, coherence)
├── clustering.py  # radius graph and connected components
├── history.py     # per-composition persistence, bounded and time-expiring
├── analyzer.py    # the one component the pipeline talks to
└── reporting.py   # schema 0.3.5 and its writer
```

One behaviour pass now fans out to three independent consumers:

```text
                       ┌→ BehaviorWriter      (schema 0.3.3)
BehaviorFrame ─────────┼→ InteractionAnalyzer (schema 0.3.4)
                       └→ SpatialClusterAnalyzer (schema 0.3.5)
```

Three boundaries are load-bearing:

**Crowd consumes behaviour snapshots, not tracks.** Its input is already
projected, so there is one projection in the process, one motion history, and no
path by which cluster code could acquire an opinion about RF-DETR, OC-SORT or
pixels.

**Crowd does not consume `InteractionFrame`.** Both layers need pairwise
distances, but interaction measures only pairs that survive its own candidate
gate. Depending on it would silently tie cluster connectivity to an unrelated
setting and make crowd output require interaction to be enabled. A distance is
one `hypot`; recomputing it is cheaper than the coupling. Both layers bottom out
in the same `MotionPoint.distance_to`, so there is no second distance
implementation.

**Cluster state never enters `BehaviorAnalyzer` or `InteractionAnalyzer`.**
Individual, pairwise and group analysis have different cardinality, different
history and different lifetimes.

Shared infrastructure was reused rather than duplicated: `SpatialContext` from
the interaction layer (rather than a competing near-identical type),
`IntervalSampler` for emission cadence, and `JsonlStreamWriter` for gzip
selection, whole-line writes and lifecycle.

## What a spatial cluster is

A set of camera-local tracklets that were **spatially connected under a geometric
rule at one instant**.

`member_count` is therefore not a count of unique real-world people. Occlusion
can split one person across several track IDs, a track ID can be reused, and
overlapping cameras are never merged. This is the same identity boundary the
whole V0.3 line holds, restated because group cardinality is the field most
likely to be misread as a headcount.

### The clustering rule, and its chosen consequence

Radius connectivity with connected components: two subjects are connected when
their separation is **at or below** `cluster_radius`; a cluster is a connected
component of at least `minimum_cluster_size` subjects.

Connectivity is transitive, so **clusters chain**. Four people standing 1.4 m
apart in a line form one cluster under a 1.5 m radius even though the outermost
two are 4.2 m apart. This is the rule, not a defect: it is what makes a queue or
a dense line register as one arrangement rather than fragmenting into pairs. The
alternative — a maximum-diameter constraint — would split every real queue and
has no non-arbitrary tie-break for which subset survives.

`test_crowd_clustering.py::TestChaining` pins this behaviour explicitly so it is
not later "fixed" into a diameter rule by someone who reads a wide cluster as a
bug.

### Minimum size of three

A two-person arrangement is already measured, better, by V0.3.4: with distance,
closing speed, relative heading and proximity duration. Reporting it again here
would duplicate that contract with a worse model. Three is configurable but
floors at two.

## Same-camera boundary

One analyzer serves one camera. Every cluster is drawn from a single frame of a
single camera, and a snapshot from another camera raises `CrowdError` rather than
being silently ignored — clustering across cameras would count one real person
twice and cluster them with themselves.

The output makes the boundary checkable rather than merely documented:
`cluster_scope: "same_camera_only"` and `identity_scope: "camera_local_tracks"`
in the header, `camera_id` at record level, and members carrying **only** a
camera-local `track_id` with no per-member camera field to join across.

Equal track IDs in two cameras' streams are not evidence of the same person, and
nothing in the contract suggests otherwise.

## Measurements

All distances are in the active space's units — metres when calibrated,
normalized frame units otherwise.

### Cluster geometry

| Measure | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| `member_count` | Connected tracklets in the component |
| `centroid` | Arithmetic mean of member positions |
| `spatial_spread` | RMS radius about the centroid |
| `mean_pair_distance` | Mean over all member pairs |
| `max_pair_distance` | The component's true extent, which chaining can make large |

`spatial_spread` and `max_pair_distance` are both reported because they disagree
in the informative case: a chained line has a modest RMS radius and a large
extent, and a reader that saw only one of them could not tell a tight knot from a
long queue.

### Local density

`local_density = (1 + neighbours_within_radius) / (π · density_radius²)`

The subject itself is counted, so a completely isolated subject has a defined,
non-zero density rather than a hole in the data. Neighbours are counted across
**every observed subject in the frame**, not only fellow cluster members —
restricting the count to members would make density a restatement of cluster
membership rather than an independent measure of the neighbourhood.

`density_radius` is deliberately a **separate setting** from `cluster_radius`.
Connectivity asks "is this person attached to that one"; density asks "how
concentrated is this neighbourhood". A good value for one is rarely a good value
for the other.

Normalized density is emitted as `subjects/normalized_area_unit`, never
`subjects/m²`. The unit area of a normalized frame has no physical size and
shrinks with depth; naming it separately is what stops a perspective-dependent
number from being read as a crowd density.

### Motion heading coherence

The mean resultant length of the members' unit motion vectors: `1.0` when every
moving member travels the same way, `0.0` when they cancel, dimensionless and
unitless by construction.

Members whose net displacement is below `min_motion_displacement` have no
meaningful direction and are excluded. When fewer than `minimum_moving_members`
(default two) remain, the field is **omitted rather than defaulted** — one moving
member has nothing to be coherent *with*, and reporting `1.0` there would be a
fabricated agreement.

### Persistence

How long this **exact** member composition has been observed continuously.

Keyed on the exact canonical member set, so a cluster that gains or loses a
member is a different composition and starts a new episode. That is strict, and
deliberately so: the looser alternative (matching on overlap) has to choose a
similarity threshold, and every choice makes persistence a judgement rather than
a measurement.

Continuity breaks after `max_observation_gap_seconds` (0.5 s). A tracker dropping
a box for a frame or two should not restart the clock; beyond that the cluster
was unobserved long enough that continuing a duration would be a claim about time
nobody watched.

### Not implemented

No group velocity, flow field, convergence rate, dispersal rate, crowd
"pressure", queue detection, formation classification, or any measure requiring
identity across cameras or across time beyond composition persistence.

## Frame summary

A `crowd_frame` record is emitted for every measured frame **even when there are
no clusters**, carrying `observed_subject_count`, `cluster_count`,
`clustered_subject_count`, `isolated_subject_count` and `largest_cluster_size`.

"Four people were observed and none were clustered" is a measurement. A reader
that saw only cluster records could not distinguish it from a frame nobody was
in.

## Configuration

```python
CrowdAnalysisConfig.for_normalized_image()  # camera-local, perspective-dependent
CrowdAnalysisConfig.for_ground_plane_metres()  # behind a calibrated projector
```

| Setting | Normalized | Metric | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `cluster_radius` | 0.12 | 1.5 m | Connectivity distance |
| `minimum_cluster_size` | 3 | 3 | Fewest connected subjects |
| `density_radius` | 0.20 | 2.0 m | Density sampling disk |
| `min_motion_displacement` | 0.01 | 0.10 m | Floor for "is moving" |
| `minimum_moving_members` | 2 | 2 | Floor for reporting coherence |
| `max_observation_gap_seconds` | 0.5 | 0.5 | Persistence continuity |
| `stale_cluster_ttl_seconds` | 5.0 | 5.0 | Composition forgetting |
| `max_tracked_clusters` | 2048 | 2048 | Hard memory ceiling |

**These are DEVELOPMENT defaults**: geometric parameters chosen so measurements
are reachable and testable. None is a validated crowding limit, safe density,
social-distance threshold or safety figure, and no measurement backs any of them.

Distance thresholds follow the active space and mixing them is silent and total —
1.5 normalized units is wider than the whole frame; a 0.12 m cluster radius would
connect only people already touching. `intended_space` is enforced by the
analyzer, exactly as in the behaviour and interaction configs.

**No threshold encodes a venue size.** A cluster radius is a local distance
between two people; it does not scale with how big the room is. A camera covering
a doorway and one covering a festival field use the same configuration.

## Runtime integration

```bash
# Normalized (camera-local) cluster measurement
atreides-vision run --input footage.mp4 --output annotated.mp4 \
  --camera-id north_door --crowd-output artifacts/crowd.jsonl

# Calibrated venue metres
atreides-vision run --input footage.mp4 --output annotated.mp4 \
  --camera-id north_door --calibration config/cameras/north_door.json \
  --crowd-output artifacts/crowd.jsonl.gz
```

`--crowd-output` enables behaviour analysis internally; neither
`--behavior-output` nor `--interaction-output` is required. All three streams can
be requested together and are written independently.

Rendering is unaffected: annotated frames are byte-identical with and without
crowd analysis enabled.

## Calibration integrity

Metric cluster geometry is derived from calibrated positions, so it inherits the
V0.3.3 fail-safe without a second policy. When the integrity monitor reports
`DRIFT_DETECTED`, the **shared** trust decision suppresses behaviour, interaction
and crowd output together, and no stale metric cluster record leaks afterward.

Analysis continues while output is suppressed — the run still reports how many
clusters were measured — because stopping measurement would lose the record of
what the pipeline was doing when it stopped being trustworthy.

`test_pipeline_crowd.py::TestCalibrationIntegritySuppression` asserts the crowd
stream cannot bypass the shared decision, including under `--integrity-report-only`
where emission deliberately continues.

## Zone context

`common_zone_ids` (zones every member is inside) and `member_zone_ids` (the union)
are attached as **context only**. Zones never influence clustering: members
sharing a zone can be at opposite ends of an entire dancefloor, so a shared zone
says nothing about how close they are, and it is never what put them in a cluster.

## Output contract

Schema `0.3.5`, versioned **independently** of behaviour (`0.3.3`) and interaction
(`0.3.4`). Clusters have different cardinality, history and lifetime from both;
tying the versions together would force a bump on one whenever another changed.

```text
{"record_type":"run_metadata","schema_version":"0.3.5","stream":"crowd",...}
{"record_type":"crowd_frame","schema_version":"0.3.5","frame_index":200,...}
{"record_type":"spatial_cluster","schema_version":"0.3.5","frame_index":200,...}
```

Members serialise in canonical order, so the same cluster always serialises
identically and two runs over the same input produce byte-identical files.

Output sampling (`--behavior-output-interval`) and gzip (a `.jsonl.gz` path)
apply to the crowd stream exactly as they do to the other two — the same
`BehaviorEmissionPolicy` and the same `JsonlStreamWriter`. **Analysis is never
sampled**: every frame is measured in full and the policy decides only whether
the result is serialised.

## Perspective validation

The argument for calibration, measured rather than asserted. The same three
people in the same physical arrangement, at 0.5 m and 8.0 m from a strongly
perspective-projecting camera:

| | Near | Far |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Normalized spread | larger | smaller (ratio > 1.5 on mean pair distance) |
| Normalized clustering, default radius | **no cluster** | **one cluster** |
| Calibrated spread | equal to 1e-4 | equal to 1e-4 |
| Calibrated clustering | one cluster of 3 | one cluster of 3 |

The second row is the blunt cost of measuring clusters in image space: at a fixed
normalized radius, one physical arrangement clusters at the back of the room and
does not at the front, because a fixed fraction of the frame covers less floor as
people approach. Calibration removes the dependence entirely, and the recovered
RMS radius matches the layout's hand-computed value to 1e-4.

## Performance

Analyzer only — no detector, no tracker, no video I/O — on a drifting lattice
dense enough that every size forms a chained cluster, metric config, 200 frames,
median per frame:

| Subjects | ms/frame | Pair comparisons | Cluster edges |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 10 | 0.082 | 45 | 9 |
| 25 | 0.405 | 300 | 58 |
| 50 | 1.473 | 1225 | 142 |
| 100 | 5.515 | 4950 | 309 |

Comparisons are exactly *n(n−1)/2*. Cost is dominated by the O(n²) graph build
plus O(k²) pairwise metrics inside each cluster, and this lattice is close to
worst case: all members end up in one giant chained component.

**These figures say nothing about real-time capability.** They exclude the
detector, which dominates end-to-end cost by orders of magnitude, and use
synthetic input.

## Privacy boundary

No facial recognition, appearance embeddings, biometric identifiers, ReID,
persistent identity, venue-person IDs or cross-camera association. No crowd risk,
density verdict, surge, crush, panic, unrest, escalation or safety signal.

A spatial cluster is a set of ephemeral camera-local tracklets that were
geometrically connected at one instant, discarded when the process exits.

Asserted on the serialised output, not merely intended:
`test_pipeline_crowd.py::TestMultiCameraBoundary` scans emitted records for
identifier fields and checks that members carry nothing joinable across cameras.

Facial recognition, biometric identifiers and persistent identity are permanent
product boundaries. Cross-camera association and venue-person IDs are absent
because V0.3.5 does not implement them — both are V1 product capabilities, and
the venue-local identity they produce is temporary and session-scoped. What the
test above guarantees is that this milestone joins nothing across cameras, not
that joining is forbidden forever.

## Real-world validation limitation

Every figure here comes from synthetic geometry and analytically-known layouts.
Nothing in this milestone has been validated against real venue footage, a real
calibration, or a real crowd. The defaults are reachable, not correct.

## Known limitations

- **Chaining makes wide clusters.** By design (see above), but a consumer reading
  `member_count` without `max_pair_distance` will misjudge the arrangement.
- **Persistence is composition-exact.** In a busy scene where membership churns
  every few frames, persistence durations stay near zero. That is honest, not a
  bug, but it makes the measure most useful for stable arrangements.
- **`member_count` is a tracklet count, not a headcount.** Occlusion inflates it;
  merged tracks deflate it.
- **Normalized densities are not comparable** between cameras, or between depths
  within one camera.
- **Density counts only detected subjects.** Missed detections lower density
  silently, and there is no confidence attached to the figure.
- **No occlusion reasoning.** A cluster measured through a pillar is measured from
  whoever was visible.
