# V0.3.4 — Person-to-Person Interaction Signals

**Status:** implemented · **Substep:** V0.3.4 of V0.3 · **Not production ready**

## Purpose

V0.3.0–V0.3.3 measured people one at a time. V0.3.4 measures how two observed
subjects relate: how far apart they are, how fast that gap is changing, how
their directions of travel compare, and how long they have stayed close.

**It measures. It does not interpret.** "0.8 m apart, closing at 1.1 m/s,
together for 3.4 s" is a measurement. Whether that is a conversation, a queue,
two strangers passing or something else is a question this milestone
deliberately cannot answer, and there is no signal for confrontation,
aggression, fighting, threat, conflict, escalation, harassment or stalking.

## Architecture

```text
tracks
  → BehaviorAnalyzer        (projection, per-subject history, individual signals)
  → BehaviorFrame           (snapshots: position + motion + zone labels)
  → InteractionAnalyzer     (compatible pairs → separation history → pairwise signals)
  → InteractionFrame
  → JsonlInteractionWriter  (separate versioned stream)
```

```text
services/vision/src/atreides_vision/interaction/
├── models.py     # SubjectPairRef, SpatialContext, snapshots, signal types
├── config.py     # thresholds, with normalized and metric factories
├── geometry.py   # pure pairwise maths
├── history.py    # bounded per-pair separation history
├── analyzer.py   # the one component the pipeline talks to
└── reporting.py  # schema 0.3.4 and its writer
```

Two boundaries are load-bearing:

**Interaction consumes behaviour snapshots, not tracks.** Its input is already
projected, so there is one projection in the process, one motion history, and no
path by which pairwise code could acquire an opinion about RF-DETR, OC-SORT or
pixels. A second, independently-derived set of positions would drift out of
agreement with the individual signals and be impossible to reconcile.

**Pairwise state never enters `BehaviorAnalyzer`.** Individual and pairwise
analysis have different cardinality, different history and different lifetimes.

Shared infrastructure was reused rather than duplicated: `IntervalSampler` for
emission cadence, `wrap_angle` from the behaviour kinematics, and a new
`JsonlStreamWriter` extracted from the V0.3.3 behaviour writer so both streams
share one implementation of gzip selection, whole-line writes and lifecycle.

## Pair identity

`SubjectPairRef` holds two camera-local subjects in a **canonical order**:
`(track_7, track_12)` and `(track_12, track_7)` construct the same value.
Without that, pair history would duplicate the moment the tracker returned its
tracks in a different order, and proximity durations would restart for no
reason.

Rejected at construction, not merely unimplemented:

* **Self-pairs.** A subject cannot interact with itself.
* **Cross-camera pairs.** Without multi-camera association, one real person
  visible in two cameras would be paired **with themselves**, producing a
  confident measurement of a relationship that does not exist.

### Same-camera boundary

Camera-local tracking identity is well defined; venue-level identity does not
exist. So `cam_A/track_7 + cam_B/track_12` is refused even when both project
into the same venue frame and land on nearly the same coordinates. This is
enforced twice — by `SubjectPairRef` and by the analyzer validating every
snapshot against its own camera — and covered by regression tests.

Cross-camera interaction is deferred until an association layer can prevent
self-pairing, double counting and false pair creation.

### Track-ID churn

If a tracker loses someone and re-acquires them as a new id, that is a **new
subject** under the current identity model. Pairs involving the old id end;
different pairs begin, with proximity duration starting from zero. Nothing
stitches them together, and the tests assert this rather than paper over it.

## Coordinate compatibility

`SpatialContext` makes the preconditions explicit:

| Space | Requires |
|---|---|
| `normalized_image` | same **camera** (frame fractions are camera-local by construction) |
| `ground_plane_metres` | same **camera**, same `venue_id`, same `surface_id` |

Metric context **must** name its venue and surface — constructing one without
them raises. Two positions on a main floor and a balcony can have nearby
`(x, y)` and be metres apart vertically; assuming a shared plane is exactly the
silent wrongness this type prevents.

`MotionPoint` already refuses cross-space subtraction; `pair_distance` restates
that as an `InteractionError` so the caller learns which comparison was invalid.
Nothing is silently skipped or coerced.

## Signals

`current` geometry (this frame) is kept separate from `signals` (derived over a
window), exactly as behaviour separates position from signals.

### Distance — current geometry

Euclidean separation between the two already-projected person anchors:

```text
distance = sqrt((x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²)
```

Units: `m` under calibration, `normalized_frame_unit` without. Never a
bounding-box centre distance, never pixels. Zero distance is valid and does not
merge subjects.

### Closing speed

```text
closing_speed = −(distance_last − distance_first) / (t_last − t_first)
```

over a 1 s window. **Positive means the gap is shrinking.** Measured across the
window's endpoints rather than between consecutive frames, because a
frame-to-frame difference divides tracker jitter by a tiny `dt` — the same
reasoning behind the behaviour layer's windowed speed. As a windowed mean, a
pair that approaches then retreats inside one window reports the *net* rate.

It is **not** the sum of individual speeds: two people walking fast in parallel
are not closing at all. Verified against five analytic cases:

| Scenario | Expected | Measured |
|---|---|---|
| B still, A approaches at 1 m/s | `+1 m/s` | `+1.0000` |
| B still, A leaves at 1 m/s | `−1 m/s` | `−1.0000` |
| Both moving the same way at 1 m/s | `0` | `0.0000` |
| Head-on, 1 m/s each | `+2 m/s` | `+2.0000` |
| Separating, 1 m/s each | `−2 m/s` | `−2.0000` |

Units: `m/s` or `normalized_frame_unit/s`.

### Relative motion heading

Unsigned angle between the two subjects' directions of travel, in `[0, π]`:
`0` same direction, `π` opposite, `π/2` perpendicular. Wrapped, so `+170°` and
`−170°` read as the 20° difference they are.

**This is direction of travel, not body orientation.** Atreides has no gaze or
posture signal. Two people standing still facing each other produce no heading
at all, because neither is moving — hence the name `relative_motion_heading`
rather than anything about facing.

Omitted when either subject has moved less than the configured floor. That is
not `0`: "neither is going anywhere" and "both going the same way" are different
facts.

Units: `rad`.

### Proximity duration

Continuous seconds while `distance <= proximity_threshold`. The boundary counts
as *inside*. Resets on crossing above the threshold, on a long observation gap,
and on pair expiry. Computed from real timestamps, never frame counts, and
carried as a spell-start so it outlives the retained history window — two people
can stand together far longer than two seconds.

Units: `s`.

### Not implemented

No radial-velocity signal was added separately: closing speed already *is* the
rate of change of separation, and a second name for the same quantity would be a
duplicate contract. No time-to-collision, no crossing prediction, and no
semantic signal of any kind.

## Configuration

`InteractionAnalysisConfig`, with two factories mirroring the behaviour config.

| Field | Normalized default | Metric default | Unit |
|---|---|---|---|
| `closing_speed_window_seconds` | `1.0` | `1.0` | s |
| `min_heading_displacement` | `0.01` | `0.10` | distance units |
| `proximity_threshold` | `0.15` | `1.5` | distance units |
| `max_observation_gap_seconds` | `0.5` | `0.5` | s |
| `history_window_seconds` | `2.0` | `2.0` | s |
| `max_pair_distance` | `None` | `8.0` | distance units |
| `stale_pair_ttl_seconds` | `5.0` | `5.0` | s |
| `max_tracked_pairs` | `4096` | `4096` | count |
| `max_samples_per_pair` | `256` | `256` | count |
| `intended_space` | `normalized_image` | `ground_plane_metres` | — |

> **DEVELOPMENT defaults.** No measurement backs them. The 1.5 m proximity
> threshold is a placeholder chosen so the signal is reachable and testable —
> **not a validated personal-space distance**, and carrying no behavioural
> meaning whatsoever. None of these is a safety threshold.

Mixing spaces is silent and total — a normalized threshold read as metres puts
proximity 15 cm away; a metric one read as frame fractions puts it ten
frame-widths away — so `intended_space` is enforced at analyzer construction,
exactly as in V0.3.3.

## Pair history and expiry

One bounded history per canonical pair. Three independent bounds, which matter
more here than for individual subjects because pair state is quadratic:

* per pair by time window and absolute sample count;
* store-wide by a 5 s time-to-live on unseen pairs;
* a hard ceiling evicting the least recently seen.

Pairs are also dropped as soon as either subject stops being tracked, so the
store stays proportional to what is on screen rather than what was on screen
five seconds ago.

**Observation gaps** follow the behaviour layer's rule: a short gap (a dropped
box or two) is bridged, because both people were still there. A longer gap
starts a new run and discards what came before — two people who disappeared for
four seconds may have parted and returned, and a proximity duration spanning
that gap would be a claim about time nobody watched. On the first frame back no
spell is claimed at all; it rebuilds from zero.

**Gated pairs keep their history.** A pair drifting outside `max_pair_distance`
is not analysed but is not deleted either, so a pair moving in and out of range
keeps its spell; time-based expiry collects it if they stay apart.

## Runtime integration

```bash
# Normalized (camera-local) interaction measurement
atreides-vision run \
  --input data/input/clip.mp4 \
  --output artifacts/tracked.mp4 \
  --interaction-output artifacts/interactions.jsonl \
  --camera-id cam_01

# Calibrated venue metres
atreides-vision run \
  --input data/input/clip.mp4 \
  --output artifacts/tracked.mp4 \
  --interaction-output artifacts/interactions.jsonl \
  --calibration config/calibration.json
```

`--interaction-output` **enables behaviour analysis internally** and does not
require `--behavior-output`: nobody should have to produce one file to get
another. Writing behaviour JSONL stays a separate choice, and the two streams
can be produced together or independently.

Interaction is opt-in. A run without the flag is byte-for-byte what it was, and
rendering is untouched — no overlay, no marker, no alert. Emission sampling
(`--behavior-output-interval`) and gzip (`.jsonl.gz`) apply to the interaction
stream through the same shared components.

## Calibration integrity

Metric interaction output honours the V0.3.3 policy through **one shared trust
decision** in the processor, not a second policy of its own:

| Integrity state | Metric interaction output |
|---|---|
| `UNCHECKED` | emitted |
| `CONSISTENT` | emitted |
| `DRIFT_DETECTED` (guarded, default) | **stopped, for the rest of the run** |
| `DRIFT_DETECTED` (report-only) | emitted, with the warning V0.3.3 already logs |
| `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE` | emitted; the behaviour stream records the state |

Pairwise metres derive from the same positions the calibration has disowned, so
output the metric pipeline has stopped trusting must not reappear as an
interaction record. Behaviour and interaction stop together, and the frame
counter counts suppression once per frame regardless of how many streams are
enabled.

Analysis continues while output is suppressed — suppression is about trust in
the output, not about stopping work.

## Zone context

`shared_zone_ids` reports zones **both** subjects are inside, in the first
subject's configuration order. Context only: two people sharing a zone are in
the same operator-drawn region, which can be an entire dancefloor and says
nothing about how close they are.

Zones are **not** used to gate pair creation. People either side of a zone
boundary can be centimetres apart, so proximity is measured geometrically and
never inferred from zone equality. Zones remain camera-local image-space labels.

## Output contract

**Schema version `0.3.4`, owned independently of the behaviour schema.** A
separate stream, because pairs have different cardinality, different history and
different lifetimes; tying the versions together would force a bump on one
whenever the other changed. The behaviour stream is untouched and stays at
`0.3.3`.

```json
{"record_type":"run_metadata","schema_version":"0.3.4","stream":"interaction",
 "camera_id":"cam_01","space":"ground_plane_metres","distance_unit":"m",
 "is_physical":true,"identity_scope":"camera_local_track_pair",
 "pair_scope":"same_camera_only","venue_id":"venue_01","surface_id":"main_floor",
 "proximity_threshold":1.5}
```

```json
{"record_type":"interaction","schema_version":"0.3.4","camera_id":"cam_01",
 "frame_index":140,"timestamp_seconds":5.6,
 "subjects":[{"track_id":7},{"track_id":12}],
 "space":"ground_plane_metres",
 "current":{"distance":{"value":0.84,"unit":"m","space":"ground_plane_metres"}},
 "signals":[{"type":"closing_speed","value":1.12,"unit":"m/s"},
            {"type":"relative_motion_heading","value":3.14159,"unit":"rad"},
            {"type":"proximity_duration","value":3.4,"unit":"s"}],
 "venue_id":"venue_01","surface_id":"main_floor",
 "shared_zone_ids":["dancefloor"]}
```

`subjects` is canonically ordered, so the same two subjects always serialise
identically. `identity_scope` and `pair_scope` are stated on the header so a
consumer cannot mistake these for venue-level pairs.

## Perspective validation

Two people a fixed **2 m** apart, measured near the camera and far from it,
through a synthetic strongly-oblique camera:

| | Normalized image distance | Calibrated ground plane |
|---|---|---|
| Near the camera | larger | **2.0000 m** |
| Far from the camera | smaller | **2.0000 m** |
| Ratio | **> 1.5×** | **1.000×** |

The same physical separation subtends very different image separations by
depth alone. Only the calibrated measurement recovers the real 2 m at both
depths — which is why metric mode is the meaningful one for interaction
distance.

## Dense-scene scaling

Pair generation is O(N²). Measured with 300 frames at 1920×1080, metric mode:

| Subjects | Theoretical pairs | Candidates/frame | Analysed/frame | Overhead |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 10 | 10 | 10 | +0.20 ms/frame |
| 10 | 45 | 45 | 45 | +1.09 ms/frame |
| 25 | 300 | 300 | 300 | +5.12 ms/frame |
| 50 | 1225 | 1225 | 1225 | +20.70 ms/frame |

Pair counts match `N(N−1)/2` exactly. Per-pair cost is stable at **≈17–24 µs**,
so the growth is entirely the quadratic pair count, not per-pair inefficiency.

Candidate-distance gating on a spread 40-subject crowd:

| Gate | Candidates/frame | Analysed/frame | ms/frame |
|---|---|---|---|
| none | 780 | 780 | 17.4 |
| 8 m | 780 | 460 | 10.8 |
| 4 m | 780 | 129 | 7.1 |

Gating is the intended bound for dense scenes. **No spatial index was built**:
at realistic per-camera counts the linear scan is not the bottleneck, and
building a tree now would be complexity without evidence. If sustained counts
above ~50 subjects per camera become normal, a uniform grid or k-d tree over the
projected positions is where it should go — the candidate loop in
`InteractionAnalyzer.update` is the single place that would change.

## Performance

Fake detector and tracker, deterministic synthetic input, **detector inference
excluded**. Interaction adds +0.20 ms/frame at 5 subjects and +20.7 ms/frame at
50 ungated, at ≈17 µs per analysed pair.

**These figures say nothing about real-time capability.** They exclude the
detector, which dominates end-to-end cost by orders of magnitude, and use
synthetic frames.

## Privacy boundary

No facial recognition, appearance embeddings, biometric identifiers, ReID,
persistent identity, venue-person ids or cross-camera association. No aggression,
intoxication, distress, suspicion, threat, conflict or risk signal of any kind.
A pair is two ephemeral camera-local tracklets, discarded when the process exits.

Asserted on the serialised output, not merely intended.

## Real-world validation limitation

**No metre ground truth exists for the downloaded CCTV footage.** V0.3.4
therefore validates pair mathematics, metric correctness under *synthetic*
calibration, perspective recovery, runtime integration, output correctness and
computational scaling.

It claims **no** real-world distance accuracy, no validated personal-space
threshold, no validated proximity semantics, and nothing whatsoever about what
any measured configuration of two people means.

## Known limitations

1. **Track-ID churn ends pairs.** An occlusion that re-ids one person destroys
   that pair's history and starts a new one at zero.
2. **No cross-camera pairs**, by design. The same person in two cameras is two
   unrelated subjects and is never paired.
3. **Metric calibration is not real-world validated** — inherited from V0.3.1.
4. **O(N²) pair scaling.** Gating bounds it; no spatial index exists.
5. **Motion heading is not body orientation.** Two people facing each other
   while stationary produce no heading at all.
6. **All thresholds are development values** with no measurement behind them.
7. **Closing speed is a windowed mean**, so approach-then-retreat inside one
   window nets out rather than showing the peak.
8. **Proximity is a plain distance test.** It cannot distinguish two people
   standing together from two people separated by a bar, a barrier or a wall —
   the ground plane has no obstacles in it.
9. **Same-surface only.** Pairs across surfaces are refused rather than
   approximated, so a person on a stage and one on the floor are never compared.
10. **Sampling is per frame, not per pair**, so a busy frame is emitted whole or
    not at all.
11. **Zone context is image-space**, inherited from V0.2.

## Verification

1534 tests pass (1335 before this milestone, 199 added), `ruff check`,
`ruff format --check` and strict `mypy` all clean.

### One narrow addition to the behaviour layer

Relative heading needs each subject's direction of travel, which the individual
signals did not expose — they carry the *rate of change* of direction, not the
direction. `BehaviorSubjectSnapshot` gained an optional `motion: SubjectMotion`
field: the net displacement over the same heading window the behaviour layer
already computes, from the same history. Deriving it a second time in the
interaction layer would have produced an estimate that could drift out of
agreement with the individual signals. The field is additive and optional, and
all 1335 prior tests pass unchanged.
